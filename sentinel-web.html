Finds Bluetooth trackers that might be following you. It scans while this page is open, notes where you were, and flags a device seen at several of your places. Everything stays in this browser.

Setup and how it works

    Turn on scanning in Chrome

    1. Use Google Chrome (not Samsung Internet).
    2. Open chrome://flags, search for experimental web platform, set Experimental Web Platform features to Enabled and relaunch.
    3. Turn on Bluetooth and Location.
    4. In Android Settings → Apps → Chrome → Permissions, allow Nearby devices and Location (precise).

    How a device gets flagged

    The same rules as the Sentinel app. Places are fixes more than 150 m apart. A device is flagged when, within 24 hours, it was seen at 3 or more of your places over at least 20 minutes and scores 55 or more. Points come from the number of places, how far apart they are, repeat visits, and whether it matches a known tracker format (Apple Find My, Samsung SmartTag, Tile, Google Find Hub, Chipolo).

    Limits: it only sees what happens while this page is open and on screen. The browser can't scan in the background, so open it at different places, or leave it open while travelling. Trackers can still be missed. Chrome hides real Bluetooth addresses, so ordinary devices can't be followed across an address change, only recognised trackers.

    Your data

    Sightings and places are kept in this browser's storage on this device for 7 days, and are never sent anywhere. The map on a device's page loads OpenStreetMap tiles, so the tile server sees which area is being viewed.

    Sentinel Web is a companion to the Sentinel Android app. It never connects to a device, only listens to what devices broadcast.
