Concept overview · not a live product

A licensing research team that never gets tired of state regulations

An assistant system that does the first pass of state-by-state money transmitter licensing research automatically — so your team spends its time reviewing and deciding, not hunting for requirements.

Please read before anything else This is a decision-support tool, not a substitute for licensed compliance or legal review. It does not make licensing determinations and it is not a source of current regulatory figures. Every recommendation it produces should be verified by your compliance team against NMLS and each state's own regulator before you act on it.
The problem

Expanding into a new state means redoing the same research, every time

Every state has its own money transmitter licensing rules — bonding requirements, minimum net worth, review timelines, exemptions. When a business wants to expand into new states, someone has to research each one, keep the findings organized, and turn that into something leadership and legal can act on. It's repetitive, easy to get inconsistent between states, and it eats hours that could go toward actually reviewing the judgment calls that matter.

The solution

A small team of assistants, each with one job

Instead of one person doing everything by hand, four narrowly-scoped assistants each handle one part of the process — the way you'd split the work across a research analyst, a checklist owner, a writer, and a second reviewer.

🔎

Research Assistant

Looks up what a given state requires — bonding, net worth, typical review time — instantly, instead of someone searching state by state.

📋

Checklist Builder

Turns a list of target states into one clear go/no-go packet: what's covered, what's missing, and what still needs real research.

✍️

Memo Writer

Drafts a short, readable summary memo from the checklist — the kind of write-up you'd hand to a stakeholder or put in front of legal.

🛡️

Second-Look Reviewer

Checks the checklist and memo for completeness before a human ever sees it — every target state accounted for, nothing silently skipped.

How it fits your workflow

1
"We're licensed in California and New York. We want to expand into Texas, Florida, and Illinois."
→

→
3

→
4
The reviewer double-checks nothing was missed, then the memo writer drafts the summary for your team.
Why it's worth it

What you get

  • A consistent first draft for every state, instead of whatever one researcher happened to find that week
  • Far less manual lookup time before your compliance team's real review even starts
  • A packet that's already been checked once before it reaches a person — nothing obviously missing
  • A clear, honest "we don't know yet" whenever a state falls outside what the assistants have researched, instead of a guess dressed up as an answer
What this is not

Judgment still belongs to your team

These assistants produce a first draft, not a final answer. They don't replace your compliance officers, your outside counsel, or your regulators — they take the repetitive research off your team's plate so the people with real judgment can spend their time on the calls that actually need it. Every packet they produce should be reviewed by a qualified person and verified against current NMLS and state-regulator sources before anyone acts on it.
