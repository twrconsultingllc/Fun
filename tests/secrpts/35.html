Review #35 — sentinel-web.html My gear

A new My gear tab for guarding your own devices, built for a triathlete's heart-rate strap, watch and bike computer. Register a device lists what's heard, strongest first, marks the closest, and names what it broadcasts. You add a name, owner, type and a photo taken inside the page. Each device then has a mode. In use alarms when it goes quiet and keeps where it was last heard. Parked arms after a quiet minute, alarms if the device wakes up, then logs every ping with the time and place. The alarm beeps, vibrates, shows a banner and, if allowed, sends a notification, until "I have it". Registered gear is never flagged as following you.

01Security & privacy

One CSP change: blob: images, for the page's own photos
Confirmed clean

Gear photos are shown from object URLs, so img-src is now 'self' blob: https://tile.openstreetmap.org. A blob: URL can only point at data this page created itself (a photo just taken, or one read back from its own storage), so this doesn't open a route to another site. connect-src 'none', script-src and everything else are unchanged. The camera preview uses srcObject, which the CSP doesn't govern. The suite asserts the exact img-src.

Photos and gear stay on the device, and "Delete all data" removes them
Confirmed clean

Photos are taken with getUserMedia inside the page, which the camera permission prompt covers. They're drawn to a canvas, saved as a JPEG of at most 1024 px, and kept in two new IndexedDB stores (gear, photos), never sent anywhere. The database upgrade to version 2 only adds those stores, and keeps existing sightings, devices, flags and settings (the version checks are in onupgradeneeded). The camera stream stops when a photo is taken, cancelled, or the screen is left. "Delete all data" clears all six stores and revokes the object URLs, and removing one device deletes its photo. In Chromium, both registered devices, the photo and the pings came back after a reload.

Your own gear is never flagged, and names are still text
Confirmed clean

Detection skips any device whose Chrome IDs belong to registered gear, and registering one removes an existing flag for it. The suite registers an AirTag on a bike and takes it to three places 3 km apart. Unregistered it would score 70 and be flagged (the Find My case from 32.html). Registered, it isn't. Names typed by the user, and advertised names, are only ever set with textContent or value, and the source grep for HTML-building calls still finds nothing. A photo's alt text is set with setAttribute.

Recognising gear by name can be fooled
Open

Watches often change their Chrome ID, so a device can also be recognised by its exact advertised name (on by default when it has one, and switchable when registering). Two people with the same model can share a name, such as "Edge 540", and anyone can advertise any name. So a Parked alarm could miss a stolen device if a same-named device is nearby, or fire because of someone else's. A name match also teaches the device a new ID (up to 10). The detail screen says how a device is recognised. Left open as a limit of what a browser can see. The Android app could match on stable data that Chrome hides.

02Code quality & accessibility

Alarm rules, tested with hand-set times
Confirmed clean

The rules are pure functions (gearOnReading, gearTick, setGearMode, gearStatus), tested at exact times:

  • In use: no alarm at 44 s quiet, "gone" just after 45 s, once, cleared when heard again. A device never heard since guarding started also counts as gone after the delay.
  • Parked: not armed 50 s after its last ping, armed after 60 s, "woke" exactly once, pings logged at most every 5 s with where they were heard.

Making Parked arm straight away turned a check red. So did removing the "never flag my gear" rule, once the test used a device that would really be flagged. Its first version used a heart-rate strap that only scores 35, which proved nothing, so the test was changed.

The whole flow in Chromium, with a fake camera
Confirmed clean

Chromium's fake camera and simulated Bluetooth, run end to end:

  • Registered "My HRM-Pro" with a photo, found as the closest device and named "Garmin · Heart rate", then "Edge 540" as Parked.
  • Stopped the strap: after the 20 s delay the banner read "My HRM-Pro has gone quiet", the tab showed 1, and the beep loop ran with audio "running".
  • The parked Edge armed, then woke on its next ping and logged 2 pings with locations. "I have it" stopped the beeping.
  • After a reload: both devices, the photo and the pings were there.

No page errors. The one console noise is map tiles this container can't download.

Card and banner status colours leaked into their text
Fixed

The gear cards and status banner used the classes ok, warn and alarm, and the page already has .ok and .warn as text colours. So a guarded device's whole card, name included, turned green or orange (seen in the first screenshot). The level classes are now lvl-ok, lvl-warn, lvl-alarm and lvl-idle, which only colour the border.

Fix: renamed the level classes.
Alarm count badge contrast
Fixed

The red alarm count on the My gear tab first used white text. On the dark theme's #ff6b6b that's below 4.5:1. It now uses the page background colour: #0b0e14 on #ff6b6b is 6.96:1, and #f5f6f9 on #c62828 is 5.2:1. Other new pairs: the "mine" tag 8.61:1 and 5.11:1, alarm status text 6.52:1 and 5.62:1, form fields 13.21:1 and 13.94:1.

Fix: .count.alarm text uses var(--bg).
A map pin hid another
Fixed

A woken device's map listed its last-heard spot first and then its pings. The last-heard spot is usually the latest ping, so pin 3 was drawn on top of pin 1. The map now lists pings in time order, and adds the last-heard spot only when it differs from the latest ping.

Fix: ordered and de-duplicated map points.
Not yet tried with real triathlon gear
Open

Still unknown: which of the user's heart-rate strap, watch and bike computer broadcast Bluetooth LE when idle or when connected to something else (ANT+ is invisible to browsers), how loud the alarm is on the tablet, and whether Samsung's Chrome allows the notification. A strap connected to a watch may go quiet and set off a false "gone". The page says guarding only works while it's open with the screen on. Left open until the user has tested it.

03Recorded for later (not built)

Background guarding and a shared race-day network
Open

The user's future idea, recorded 2026-09-27. In an Android app: guarding runs passively in the background, which the Sentinel app's foreground service already makes possible. A shared network: everyone at a triathlon or marathon who runs the app quietly watches for everyone's registered gear, so a stolen device that's moving is reported and followed through the crowd. Designing that later will need what Apple's and Google's finding networks do. Reports should be encrypted to the owner, so the crowd can't see whose gear is where. Identifiers should rotate, so the network can't be used to track a person rather than a device. And the server should never learn anyone's location history. Not started. The page's Setup panel mentions it under "Coming later".
