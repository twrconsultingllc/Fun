Review #64 — J.A.R.V.I.S. Session 12: meaning module

Say "upgrade your brain" and the test copy downloads a language module from this site (MediaPipe's text embedder and the Universal Sentence Encoder, about 12 MB) while its neural network lights up. From then on, a sentence nothing else understood is compared by meaning with a few example sentences for each command, with the phrases you taught it and with your protocols' names. Very close: he does it. Somewhat close: "Did you mean …?". Otherwise, the usual line. It's a safety net only: every command that worked before works the same.

01Security and privacy

Head block and CSP unchanged
Confirmed clean

git diff of jarvis-test.html touches no <meta> line except the description. The suite pins the whole CSP string to what it was before Session 8, now from the Session 12 section too. The module needs only what the hand tracker already uses: script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval' for the bundle and its WebAssembly, and connect-src 'self' for the model. In Chromium over the local server, every request the upgrade made went to the page's own origin, and no CSP violation was logged.

Self-hosted and pinned by hash
Confirmed clean

jarvis/text/ holds four unmodified files: text_bundle.js, text_wasm_internal.js and text_wasm_internal.wasm from npm @mediapipe/tasks-text@0.10.14 (the same version as the hand tracker's vision package; its npm integrity was sha512-hQU/t9df…), and universal_sentence_encoder.tflite (float32, v1) from MediaPipe's model bucket, 6.1 MB. Apache-2.0. The README lists each file's source and SHA-256, and the suite checks all four hashes and that the README lists them. Only the SIMD WebAssembly build is included, as in jarvis/hands/. Like the hand tracker, the bundle is loaded with import(), which can't carry an SRI attribute; the pinned hashes in the suite are the check, as they are for jarvis/hands/.

Never loaded at start-up
Confirmed clean

The bundle is imported in one place, textLib, which only upgradeBrain() calls (both pinned in the suite). In Chromium, the request log after loading the page and skipping the boot holds nothing from jarvis/text/; after "jarvis upgrade your brain please" it holds the bundle, the loader, the WebAssembly and the model. A changed page that loaded the module at start-up failed the suite.

Browser cache, not the Cache API
Confirmed clean

Decided in the session. The files are fetched normally, so the browser's own cache keeps them, and a second upgrade in a later visit is quick. Keeping them in the Cache API would be a new kind of stored data, 12 MB of it, with nothing in the page to clear it except clearing site data, and offline use is Session 15's job. In Chromium, caches.keys() is empty after an upgrade.

Nothing new is stored
Confirmed clean

The fingerprints (100 numbers per sentence) and their middle point live in memory, are worked out on each upgrade, and are gone on reload. The one-place-that-saves checks pass unchanged: no localStorage write anywhere, exactly one 'readwrite' transaction, in flush(), and Session 12 adds no save( call. In jsdom, after the upgrade and every kind of match, a database dump holds the same five tables at version 2, only listed keys in kept and meta, no list of numbers, no example sentence and nothing said to the module. In real IndexedDB in Chromium, the database before and after the upgrade and three matched turns differed only in the usage counts, and was still at version 2.

Destructive commands are exact only
Confirmed clean

Two guards. No command in the meaning list deletes, forgets, resets or reboots (the suite checks the list and its examples against DESTRUCTIVE), and a taught phrase whose meaning does is left out of the candidates. And anything said with such a word (forget, delete, remove, erase, wipe, clear, reset, undo, scrap, ditch, get rid of, throw away, cancel, reboot, power down…) is never matched at all, however close: "forget about the globe" at 0.99 gets the usual "didn't understand". The word list was widened in the session, after "get rid of the movie night thing" turned out to be a way of saying delete that the first list missed. Deleting a protocol still needs its exact command and a yes.

The camera and mic are never started on a guess
Confirmed clean

A match for the threat scan, hand control, always listening or scanning a settings code always asks first, even at 0.97. These are worked out from the commands themselves (asksFirst()), so a taught phrase meaning "scan the room" asks too. The same rule as Session 10's protocols: the camera only starts when you ask.

No user text as markup
Confirmed clean

"Did you mean …?" can name a taught phrase or a protocol, both already normalised to lower-case letters, digits, apostrophes and spaces, and shown through add(), which builds text nodes. The suite's "no innerHTML anywhere" check passes. A sentence that looks personal is never compared, and nothing compared is kept.

npm audit: source-map-js
Open

Carried from review 61. No test dependency was added; the model files are served to the page, not installed.

02Code quality

A safety net: the patterns always win
Confirmed clean

The module is asked last in answerCore(), after respond() (so after intent() and brainKnown()), a protocol name, a fragment and "wake up". To pin it, the suite opens two windows, one with a brain that never matches and one with a brain that thinks everything means "tell me a joke", and types the same 78 phrases into both: every command link, plus unpunctuated phrasings, settings, short-term memory, delete, forget and reset commands. Every answer is identical, and the always-a-joke brain was never reached. A page that asked the module first failed it.

The bands, set against the real model
Confirmed clean

Raw Universal Sentence Encoder fingerprints for short commands are all alike: in a calibration run of 205 sentences in Chromium, nearly every pair scored above 0.9, so "where is my phone" scored 0.956 against "what time is it". Measured from the middle of the example sentences (worked out on each upgrade, never stored), they spread out: of 29 rewordings, 25 ranked their own command first, and 25 sentences that mean nothing he can do scored 0.28 to 0.70. So: 0.80 or more, and at least 0.05 ahead of the next, does it; 0.60 or more asks; less is the usual line. Of the unrelated sentences, three land in the "Did you mean" band ("what is love", 0.70, asks about a heart in particles; "play some music" and "sing me a song" ask about always listening) and none in the "do it" band. "Show me the planet we live on" scores 0.821 against the globe in the page, 0.11 ahead of the next.

"Did you mean …?" through ask()
Confirmed clean

Pinned with real turns, typed into the input: "yes" runs it and counts it; "no" runs nothing and asks what you meant, so the next thing you say teaches him the phrase, as usual; anything else is answered as itself and drops the question, so a "yes" after it answers nothing. Two candidates within 0.05 of each other ask rather than pick.

Counted under their own IDs; one new ID
Confirmed clean

A matched command runs through respond() with its own command text, so it counts as itself (scene:globe, cmd:joke, cmd:protocol…), both straight away and after "yes". Asking counts nothing. "Upgrade your brain" counts once as cmd:upgrade (44 usage IDs now) and has a CAPABILITIES entry, which needs only WebAssembly. It isn't a clearance extra, decided in the session: it helps him understand you, which a newcomer needs most, so it's open from level 1.

Phone speech, and everything through say()
Confirmed clean

"Upgrade your brain" is matched on normPhrase() with "jarvis", "hey jarvis", "please", "can you" in front and "please", "now", "jarvis" after; "jarvis upgrade your brain please", "Jarvis, upgrade your brain." and "please upgrade your brain jarvis" are pinned. Rewordings are compared the same way, so "jarvis show me the planet we live on please" is the same sentence to the module. The two lines said later ("Language comprehension module online." and the failure line) go through say(), and the download starts after the first answer has been said, so a fast result can't come before it.

The suite fails when the code is broken
Confirmed clean

Fourteen changes were run against the suite. All fourteen failed it: asking the module before the patterns (5 checks), no destructive check on what was said (2), destructive taught phrases as candidates (8 or more), the camera and mic not asking (4), no gap check (2), no "Did you mean" band (8 or more), "no" running it anyway (8 or more), loading at start-up (1), fingerprints in localStorage (2), matched commands not counted (7), the upgrade only understood punctuated (8 or more), a wider CSP (5), the "online" line not through say() (2), and a loose "delete a protocol" candidate (3). In the first pass the gap check wasn't caught: the test's near-tie couldn't score above 0.7 with its made-up fingerprints, so it asked for the wrong reason. It's now a real near-tie (0.90 against 0.88).

Some rewordings are still missed
Open

By design, a miss is cheaper than a wrong guess. In the calibration run, "show me the red planet" ranked the globe above Mars, "turn into morpheus" and "i want to see the world" scored under 0.6, and "how is your day going" ranked the date first (0.62, so it would ask). More example sentences would help, and are cheap to add; each one moves the middle point, so the bands should be checked again in Chromium when the list changes.
