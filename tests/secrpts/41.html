Review #41 — jarvis.html: the mic stopped transcribing

A fix for a regression shipped in 40.html. The user reported that after that update, tapping the mic showed LISTENING but nothing was ever transcribed. The cause was the risk review 40 left open: a second mic stream, opened so the orb could follow your loudness, took the mic away from speech recognition. The orb now reacts through speech recognition's own speechstart and speechend events, and the page never opens a mic stream of its own.

01The regression

Speech recognition heard nothing
Fixed

From review 40, recognition's onstart called getUserMedia({audio:true}) and fed the stream into an AnalyserNode. On the user's phone, that stream got the mic and recognition got silence: it showed LISTENING, then ended with no result. Review 40 marked this as a possibility (Open). Its guard only covered a device that reports audio-capture, and this phone failed silently. The level meter (startMeter, stopMeter, rmsLevel, levelToEnergy) is gone. The listening path is back to what it was before review 40, plus two event handlers. A grep finds one getUserMedia call left in the page, hand control's camera request, which asks for audio:false.

Fix: rec.onspeechstart/onspeechend set a hearing flag. While it's set, the orb's energy jumps to 0.95 and flutters between 70% and 100% of it (orbPulse({hearing})). The flag clears on onend, onerror and onstart.
A test that would have caught it
Fixed

Review 40's suite checked that the second stream opened and closed correctly. It treated opening the stream as intended, so it couldn't catch this. The suite now records every getUserMedia call through a fake recognizer's full cycle: tap, start, speech, result, end, error. It requires zero calls. Against review 40's code that check fails, because onstart made one call. It also checks that a spoken transcript reaches the chat log, and that the hearing flag follows the speech events and clears on end and on error.

Checked in Chromium
Confirmed clean

At 1280×800 and 390×844, with getUserMedia wrapped to count calls and a fake recognizer whose start() throws when already running (as the real one does; see CLAUDE.md): the orb's energy was 0.43 while listening quietly and 0.95 after speechstart. A spoken "what is seven times eight" was answered "That would be 56." The page made 0 calls to getUserMedia. No page errors, no CSP violations. The CSP and the rest of review 40's changes (mic note, HUD strip, word pulse, reduced motion) are unchanged.

02Still open

Real speech recognition on the user's phone
Open

Headless Chromium has no working speech service, so recognition itself was faked here, as in review 40. The fix removes the only thing that changed in the listening path, but the proof is the user talking to it on the same phone. The orb reacts when recognition says it hears speech, not to how loud you are. It is a coarser reaction than review 40 aimed for, and it's the one that doesn't compete for the mic.
