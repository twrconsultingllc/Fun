Review #38 — jarvis.html hand control

The holo-projector can now be steered with your hands through the camera. Say "hand control" or tap ✋ HANDS. A small preview shows the camera with a tracked skeleton over each hand, and a reticle follows your fingertips. Pinch and drag turns the hologram. Pinch with both hands and pull apart zooms. A quick pinch selects (a planet, say). Flicking an open hand moves to the next shape or planet. A fist crushes the particle sculpture into a ball until you open your hand. Tracking uses Google's MediaPipe hand landmarker, served from this site.

01Security & privacy

The camera stays on the device, enforced by the CSP
Confirmed clean

The tracker, its WebAssembly runtime and its model are files in this repo (jarvis/hands/), not a CDN. So connect-src went from 'none' to 'self', not to any outside host, and the page can't send a frame, a landmark or anything else off the site. The only other change is 'wasm-unsafe-eval' in script-src. That lets the page compile WebAssembly and nothing more: eval and new Function stay blocked, and a grep of the WebAssembly loader finds neither. Chromium ran the whole hand-control flow under the real CSP, served over HTTP, with zero CSP violations. The only outside hosts it touched were Google Fonts and cdnjs (three.js).

Vendored files are unmodified and pinned
Confirmed clean

The files are from npm @mediapipe/tasks-vision@0.10.14 (Apache-2.0) and Google's published hand_landmarker.task (float16, v1). They're byte-identical to the originals. The one change is that vision_bundle.mjs is renamed .js, so any static host serves it with a JavaScript MIME type. jarvis/hands/README.md lists where each came from and its SHA-256. The suite recomputes all four hashes and fails if any file changes or the README drifts. They load only when hand control is turned on, about 17 MB once, then cached.

The camera turns off when you're done
Confirmed clean

Saying "stop hand control", tapping ✋ HANDS again, closing the projector or pressing Esc all stop every camera track and clear the video element. Switching scenes keeps hand control on, on purpose. Checked in Chromium: after each of these, no tracks were live and the page was out of hands mode. The camera is only asked for when the user turns hand control on, never at load.

Each camera failure gets the right message
Fixed

At first every camera error said "couldn't find a camera", including a refused permission. Errors are now told apart by name. Permission refused: "I need permission… The video never leaves this device." No camera: "couldn't find a camera". Camera in use: "seems to be busy in another app". Anything else: "couldn't start the camera". Each was checked in Chromium by making getUserMedia reject with that error. In each case the projector stayed open, hand mode stayed off and the normal hint came back.

Fix: messages chosen by DOMException name.

02Code quality & accessibility

Real hand tracking, through a fake camera
Confirmed clean

Chromium's fake camera was fed a video made from MediaPipe's sample photo of two raised hands. The real model, running from jarvis/hands/, found both hands and labelled them Left and Right. The preview drew both skeletons in the right places, and the reticle appeared. The classifier read both as open hands (4 fingers out, pinch ratio 0.37–0.48, well above the 0.26 pinch threshold).

Gestures, tested with synthetic hands
Confirmed clean

The gesture logic is two pure functions (classifyHand, createGestures), tested with hand-built landmark sets. Open, fist and pinch are told apart. A curled fist's index finger isn't mistaken for a pinch, because a pinching index reaches past its knuckle. A quick pinch is a tap, but a pinch that moved isn't. Dragging rotates in the hand's direction. Two hands pulling apart zoom in and don't also rotate. A flick left is "next", but a slow drift isn't a swipe. A fist held 150 ms squeezes, and opening the hand releases it. The cursor sits midway between the thumb and index tips. Each action was then fed through the live page: swipes changed sphere → heart → rocket → heart and Sun → Mercury, a tap on the centre picked the Sun, a fist crushed the heart and opening rebuilt it, and rotate and zoom moved the camera.

HUD buttons overlapped on phones
Fixed

The first phone screenshot had the hint running under the HANDS button and preview, and the title under CLOSE. Then the phone rules lost to the later #holo-close rule, and measuring the boxes found the two buttons still overlapping. On phones the buttons now sit side by side, the title and stats stop short of them, and the hint stops short of the preview. Measured in Chromium at 390×844, 360×640 and 1280×800: no HUD element overlaps another.

Fix: phone layout, with higher-specificity rules.
The hand loop crashed on unexpected input
Fixed

The loop read .on from the grab action without checking it existed. That threw every frame when a test replaced the gesture engine, and would do the same if the engine ever changed shape. It now checks first.

Fix: guarded the lookup.
Commands and contrast
Confirmed clean

"Hand control", "use my hands" and "turn on gesture control" turn it on. "Stop hand control" and "turn off the hand tracking" turn it off. "Give me a hand" isn't a command, and "stop" alone still closes the projector. New text: the pressed button #020a06 on #39ff88 is 15.10:1, and the preview label is #39ff88 on a 55% dark overlay. The reticle is a pointer, not text. Boot diagnostics now report whether the browser can use a camera.

Thresholds and speed not yet tried on real hands
Open

Software-rendered Chromium managed about one detection a second, so a real flick, pinch-drag or two-hand zoom couldn't be run end to end here. Only the gesture logic (synthetic) and the page wiring (injected actions) were tested. The thresholds (pinch below 0.26 of palm size and released above 0.42, a swipe of 22% of the frame in 0.4 s, a 150 ms fist) are reasonable starting points that need tuning against the user's own hands, camera and lighting. Also unmeasured: detection speed on the user's phone or tablet. It uses the GPU and falls back to CPU, but runs on the main thread.

17 MB of binaries in the repo
Open

Self-hosting is what lets the CSP guarantee the camera stays local, but it adds 17 MB to git history permanently, and any update adds more. Loading the same files from a CDN would avoid that. But it would mean opening connect-src to outside hosts, and importing an ES module without SRI. Left open as a deliberate trade-off.
