Review #80 — J.A.R.V.I.S.: why his live voice fell silent, and the fix

On the first live try of Session 18 (review 79), the owner heard only the phone's voice. Google answered, but the proxy refused its audio with a 502 that the log couldn't explain, and the page fell back without a word. This review covers the fix: a reader that accepts the real answer's likely shapes, a log line that says why, shorter live pieces with longer waits, and a visible note whenever he falls back.

01Security and privacy

What the live try showed
Confirmed clean

On the owner's phone, /api/status answered "voice": true, and "voice samples" didn't say the supercharger was needed, so the page asked for the live voice. Vercel's log for that request read {event: "voice", model: "gemini-3.8-flash-tts", in: 128, out: 895}, and the response was 502. The proxy logs those token counts only after Google answers successfully. So the model was found in Google's live list and Google answered. What refused the answer was readVoice(), the proxy's own check, which review 79 had called clean on the strength of a fake built to the format as documented.

Reading the real answer
Fixed

The first reader took one exact shape: the first inlineData part, a media type of audio/L16 or audio/pcm, and standard padded base64. Which of these the real answer broke can't be known, because the 502's reason never reached a log.

Fix: readVoice() now takes audio from every part in order, under inlineData or inline_data. It accepts either base64 alphabet, padded or not, with line breaks, and any 16-bit PCM label (24 kHz unless a rate is named). It passes a WAV, MP3, Ogg, WebM, AAC, FLAC or MP4 file through with its own type. It still refuses anything that isn't audio, no media type at all, data that isn't base64, more than 4 MB (under Vercel's 4.5 MB response limit) and a rate outside 8–48 kHz. The recorder shares the reader and sends a passed-through file to ffmpeg as it is.
A refusal now says why, without the words
Fixed

Review 79 kept the log to the model and token counts so nothing said would ever be logged. That also hid the one fact needed here.

Fix: A refused answer now logs error: "format" with detail (which check), mime (what Google called its audio, cut to 60 characters), parts and bytes. A check sends a request with known words and confirms that none of them reaches the log.
Rate limit raised to fit shorter pieces
Confirmed clean

A long answer is now several requests, so the per-address limit for /api/voice went from 20 to 40 lines a minute (300 per instance). It is still separate from the 12 questions a minute, and the other gates (origin, on only while the supercharger is, 600 characters) are unchanged and still checked.

Whether the real answer is now read
Open

Still not testable from the container: Google can't be reached. If the real answer is something the new reader still refuses, the Vercel log will now name the check and the media type on the first try.

02Code quality

Too slow for one wait
Fixed

895 output tokens is about 35 seconds of speech for the 230-character sample answer. The page sent it as one request and waited 8 seconds, so even a readable answer would have come too late.

Fix: Live lines go in pieces of at most 160 characters (the size the browser voice already uses). All pieces start at once and play in order. The first piece of an answer is waited for 12 seconds, and each later one up to 25 (Vercel allows the function 30). A piece that arrives after its wait is still kept on the device if it may be, so a repeat plays at once.
The fallback was silent
Fixed

The page fell back to the phone's voice on any failure without a word, which made this impossible to see on the phone.

Fix: When he meant to use his own voice and didn't, his chat line gets an amber tag: "BROWSER VOICE ·" then "GEMINI VOICE FAILED", "TOO SLOW", "BUSY", "UNREACHABLE", "SWITCHED OFF", "COULD NOT PLAY IT", "RECORDING MISSING", "TAP TO ALLOW SOUND" or "LIVE VOICE RESTING AFTER FAILURES". The usual fallbacks, with the supercharger off or the browser voice chosen, get no tag. The tag reuses the amber of the existing brain tags, so its contrast is unchanged.
Three failures ended his voice for the whole visit
Fixed

After three failures in a row the page stopped asking for the rest of the visit, so a fix on the proxy wouldn't have been heard without a reload.

Fix: The live voice now rests for two minutes, then tries again. The note says it's resting.
Review 79's "verified" was too strong
Partial

Review 79 marked the response checks clean, against a fake that matched the documented shape. They failed on the first real answer. As in CLAUDE.md's lesson from the full brain's errors, a fake shows only what its author believed. The new checks feed the reader every shape a real answer might reasonably take, and the log now covers the case nobody predicted. Partial, because the real shape is still unseen.
