01Security

Nothing comes from another host
Confirmed clean

The plan asked for coastlines that the page doesn't fetch from outside. They are Natural Earth's 1:110m land, which is public domain. I took them from the world-atlas 2.0.2 npm package and converted them once, with the committed jarvis/earth/build-land.mjs, into jarvis/earth/land-110m.json (46 KB, 126 rings, 5,114 points). The page fetches that file from its own site, so connect-src 'self' covers it. A diff of the CSP meta tags of jarvis.html and jarvis-test.html is empty. In Chromium, at both sizes, every request was logged: none went anywhere except this site, cdnjs (three.js) and Google Fonts, as before. There were no Refused messages. NASA imagery wasn't used, as the plan said.

The data file is pinned and its source recorded
Confirmed clean

jarvis/earth/README.md names the source, Natural Earth 4.1.0's terms (public domain) and world-atlas's ISC licence, how to rebuild the file, and the sha256 hashes of the file and of the two npm tarballs it was built from. The suite checks the file's hash against a constant and against the README, the same way it does for the hand tracker. So a changed file fails until both are updated on purpose.

Text stays text; input only picks from a fixed table
Confirmed clean

A grep of jarvis-test.html still finds 0 innerHTML, insertAdjacentHTML, eval( or new Function. The land file is read with r.json() and used only as numbers for geometry. A spoken or typed place name is only ever used as a lookup key in PLACES, so what you say can't reach the screen except as one of the fixed names. Every answer goes through say(), which uses textContent. The night shading is a ShaderMaterial whose GLSL is two fixed strings in the page.

02Code quality & accessibility

Florida faces the camera after "show me Florida"
Confirmed clean

This is the plan's named check, done two ways. In the suite, faceAngles puts the camera direction along latLonVec(Florida) (dot product 1 to 12 places). The suite also pins makeRig's camera formula, which that result depends on. In Chromium, after the flight, Florida's point projected to (640, 400) of a 1280×800 view and (195, 422) of a 390×844 one, the exact centres, with its surface normal facing the camera (dot product 1.0000). Under reduced motion the camera jumps straight there (Sydney: az 2.639, el −0.592, as asked) instead of swooping.

The Sun's position was off by a degree
Fixed

The first version used the simple textbook declination formula. Its expected values came from astronomy, not from the page: the 2026 March equinox is at 14:46 UTC on the 20th, so at noon the Sun should be about 0.05° south of the equator. The simple formula gave 1.01° south, and the test failed. It's now the Astronomical Almanac's low-precision solar position (mean longitude and anomaly, then the ecliptic), accurate to about 0.01°. It gives 23.435°N and 0.46°E at noon UTC on the June solstice (the equation of time is −1.6 min), 23.435°S and 0.48°W in December, 0.04°S and 1.87°E at the March noon, and 0.002° from the equator at the moment of the equinox. Sunrise and sunset are checked against Orlando's published times on 21 June (6:28 a.m. and 8:27 p.m. EDT). Chromium showed the line between day and night just reaching the US east coast at about 22:20 UTC on 9 October, which is right for the time of the run.

Land data checks
Confirmed clean

Against the real file: Florida, Paris, the Sahara, Tokyo and Antarctica are land. The mid-Atlantic, the mid-Pacific, the Mediterranean and the Caspian Sea (a hole in the land rings) are water. No coastline segment jumps across or runs along the ±180° seam, where the data was cut to lie flat, and every segment is under 10° long. 226 of the 243 cities (93%) are inside a land ring. The other 17 are coastal cities the coarse coast puts just offshore, plus La Paz, inside the coarse outline of Lake Titicaca. A tap checks for a city within 2.5° first, so this doesn't show.

New commands don't take over old ones
Confirmed clean

"show me Earth", "show me the world" and "spin the globe" open the globe. "show me Florida", "take me to Tokyo", "where is Paris?", "fly to Washington D.C.", "zoom in on New York" and "show me the USA" fly there. "take me to Mars" and "take me to Earth" still go to the solar system. "make a globe" is still a particle sphere, and "show me a heart" still a sculpture. "where is my phone" and "show me the world's tallest tower" are not places. A place only matches if all of the words after the verb are a known name, not just part of them. All of these are in the suite, now 264 checks (201 before).

Still open
Open

All rendering here was software WebGL, so the frame rate on a phone is unmeasured. The globe draws 5,114 coastline segments, two 64×48 spheres and 243 lights, which is far lighter than the galaxy. Ocean names come from rough boxes, so a tap close to where two oceans meet may name the wrong one. A country or state is placed at an approximate centre, so a tap on land with no city near says "in or near" the closest one. It doesn't trace borders. These are fine for a toy, but they aren't exact.
