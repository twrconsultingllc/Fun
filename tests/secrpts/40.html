01Security & privacy

The mic level stream stays on the device and closes with listening
Confirmed clean

The level stream is getUserMedia({audio:true, video:false}) into a MediaStreamSource and an AnalyserNode. Nothing connects it to the speakers, records it or stores it. The page reads 1,024 samples a frame to get one loudness number. The CSP is unchanged (connect-src 'self'), so the page still can't send anything off the site. The stream opens only from recognition's onstart, after a tap on the mic, and is stopped in onend and onerror. If listening ends while the stream is still opening, the stream is stopped as soon as it arrives. A second jsdom window with a fake recognizer, getUserMedia and AudioContext pins each of these: one audio-only request per listen, the track stopped on end, a late stream stopped at once and never used. In Chromium, with the real fake-device flags, the state went from open to closed when the mic was tapped again.

The level stream is a second claim on the mic
Fixed in 41

Desktop Chrome lets speech recognition and a page stream share the mic. Some phones may not. If recognition reports audio-capture while the level stream is open, the page stops the stream and doesn't open it again that visit, so the next tap works with the old sine pulse. The test suite covers that path. But a phone that quietly hands the mic to one of them, with no error, would need a real device to see. That is the You step of Session 1: talk to it on your phone. If the mic stops understanding you there, this is the cause.

Update: it was. The user's phone stopped transcribing. The level stream was removed, and the orb now follows speech recognition's own events. See 41.html.

02Code quality & accessibility

Loudness to energy, checked against first principles
Confirmed clean

levelToEnergy maps RMS on a decibel scale: −50 dB is 0 and −12 dB is 1. The suite derives its expected values: 10−50/20 = 0.00316 gives 0, 10−31/20 = 0.0282 gives 0.5, 10−12/20 = 0.251 gives 1. It also checks that the result never goes below 0 or above 1, that louder never means less energy, and that NaN gives 0. In Chromium, a generated WAV (3 s at about −73 dB, then 3 s of a 220 Hz tone peaking at −9 dB) fed through --use-file-for-fake-audio-capture held the orb's energy at 0.20 in the quiet part and 0.92–1.00 in the loud part, at both 1280×800 and 390×844. The listening floor of 0.2 is deliberate, so the rings never go dead while it's listening. No page errors and no CSP violations.

Word pulses while speaking, with a fallback
Partial

The utterance's onboundary handler records the start of each word, and the pulse is 0.45 + 0.55·e−7s of the energy, where s is the time since that word started. Before any word arrives, or where a voice never reports words, the old sine pulse runs. The maths is pinned in the suite. Headless Chromium has no speech voices, so a real word-by-word pulse wasn't seen. Chrome's online voices are known not to fire onboundary, so on those the fallback is what you'll see.

Reduced motion still pulses (from review 36)
Fixed

With reduce set, the pulse is a fixed share of the energy, ring sizes ignore it, and the level stream is never opened. Brightness still eases between states over about a second, which shows what Jarvis is doing without moving anything. Chromium with reducedMotion: 'reduce': two orb frames 0.7 s apart were byte-identical while idle and while listening, and the meter stayed closed. The same check without reduced motion gave different frames, which shows the check can tell the two apart.

Fix: orbPulse({still}) and R=W*r when reduce is set.
