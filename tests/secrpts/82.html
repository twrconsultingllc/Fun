Review #82 — His own voice, faster

Follow-up to Session 18. You heard Charon live, but he was slow to start. One 230-character line came back as 895 audio tokens, about 36 seconds at Google's 25 tokens a second, twice a natural pace. Three changes, in jarvis-test.html and one proxy deploy. The styles now ask for a natural pace. The proxy streams Google's audio (streamGenerateContent) to the page as raw PCM, and the page plays it as it arrives. And an answer's first live piece is its first sentence only. Recordings were left for later, at your request.

01Security

The streamed route keeps every guard of the old one
Confirmed clean

Streaming changes only how Google is asked and how the audio comes back. The checks before it are the same code: the origin, the switch, the key, the voice rate limit, the 600-character request, and the model from the live list. The suite's "off", "no key", "another site", "no Origin" and "601 characters" checks now count a streamed call as a call to Google too, and still see none. The key goes only in x-goog-api-key, never in the ?alt=sse URL. The log gains chars, first, ms, bytes, stream and why (a fixed phrase such as status 404), and never the words: a check sends a line containing "Hidden" and searches every log entry for it. A streamed line passes on at most the same 4,000,000 bytes (about 80 seconds) as before. A 429 or a blocked line on the stream is answered as it is, so a quota or a refusal is never paid for twice. The page's CSP is unchanged: the audio still comes only from the proxy address connect-src already names, and audio/L16 is a CORS-safelisted Content-Type, so the page can read it.

RESULTS_PROXY
A line cut short is never kept on the device
Fixed

Found while building it. The first version ended a stream cleanly when Google broke off mid-line, so the page saw a complete answer and cached a cut-off line as if it were whole. The proxy now throws when a stream is cut, and the Vercel adapter answers that by breaking the connection (res.destroy()) instead of res.end(). The page then plays what came but never keeps it. A second case: when a new answer interrupted a streaming line, cancelling the read counted as "all arrived". A cancelled feed now always ends false. Both have checks, and each failed when its fix was undone (below).

02Code quality

Streaming can only make him faster, never quieter
Confirmed clean

Before the first audio arrives, anything odd falls back to the old request, one WAV, and the log says why. That covers: no streaming for the model (404), a stream with no audio, an audio file instead of PCM, a 200 with nothing readable, a network error, and a silly rate. The proxy waits for the first audio before answering, so every error before it still reaches the page as a proper status, and the page's 12-second first-piece wait and "BROWSER VOICE · …" notes work as before. A real ReadableStream cut mid-event and mid-line is put back together. Raw PCM in odd-sized pieces is carried a byte at a time to keep whole 16-bit samples, on both the proxy and the page. That also turned up a real bug before it shipped: readVoice() dropped every streamed piece's odd last byte, which would have shifted every later sample into noise. It now takes {odd: true} for streamed pieces.

RESULTS_PAGE
Played as it arrives, in real Chromium
Confirmed clean

Step 13 now runs the proxy's streamed answer through the page in headless Chromium with real Web Audio. It plays as audio/L16, the orb follows its loudness, it shows SPEAKING then STANDBY, and the one-second tone plays once, in full. With streaming refused (404), the old WAV still plays. Playwright can only hand the page a whole body, so a second check gives the page's own fetch a stream sending 1.5 seconds of tone in six pieces, a quarter of a second apart: sound started at 24 ms, and the last piece arrived at 1,518 ms. Step 13: 20 checks, no page errors, no CSP violations.

RESULTS_CHROMIUM
Whether Vercel and Google stream for real
Open

The container can't reach Google or *.vercel.app, and couldn't open Google's docs: the streaming route was confirmed from search-result summaries of Google's speech-generation page, not the page itself. Two things only a live listen can show. Whether gemini-3.8-flash-tts streams: Vercel's log says "stream":true with a first time if it does, or "stream":false and a why if it doesn't, in which case he works the old way. And whether Vercel passes the stream on as it's written: if it holds it to the end, he still speaks, just without the head start. The pace change is measurable in the same log: out / 25 seconds of speech for chars characters should now be near 15 characters a second, against about 6.4 before.
