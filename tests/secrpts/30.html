01Security

No new hosts, and the hero uses the same checks as the cards
Confirmed clean

The hero shows items from the existing nasa-iotd feed (NASA Image of the Day), which the fetcher already pulled. Its pictures are on www.nasa.gov, which was already in all three image allowlists. The hero only uses items that already passed the page's own safeLink() and safeImage(), so an off-list host, an http: picture or a javascript: link can never reach it. The CSP, feeds.config.json and the fetcher are unchanged. The suite feeds in an Image of the Day item with an off-list host, one with no picture, and one with a hostile title plus a javascript: link. The hero counts only the three good items (1 / 3) and window.__pwned stays unset.

Hero markup is built as text, never parsed
Confirmed clean

The caption, link and arrows are created with createElement and textContent, and the arrows use data-step plus one delegated listener. Grepping the page for innerHTML, outerHTML, insertAdjacentHTML, document.write and inline on*= handlers finds nothing (the existing suite assertions). The link opens in a new tab with rel="noopener noreferrer" and referrerPolicy="no-referrer", and the image also sends no referrer. The suite asserts all three.

Pictures that fail to load
Confirmed clean

If the hero picture fails to load, the page drops it and shows the next one. With one picture left the arrows go away, and when none are left the hero shrinks to a compact starfield band with just the masthead. Checked in jsdom by firing error three times in a row, and in headless Chromium with every outside request blocked: no page errors, a 240px compact hero, and the grid right below it.

02Code quality & accessibility

Hero text contrast, worst case measured
Confirmed clean

The hero text is always white, whatever the theme, over a dark scrim measured in px from the top and bottom edges. Measuring in px keeps the scrim behind the text at every hero height. The first version used percentages, and at 1440×700 its kicker dropped to 2.22:1 over a white photo, so that version was replaced before commit. Worst case, meaning a pure-white photo pixel behind each line's top and bottom edge, measured from real element positions in headless Chromium at 1440×1000, 390×844, 1440×700, 1440×500 and 800×1400:

  • small text (the ← Fun link, date, Updated stamp, kicker, summary, counter): at least 5.8:1. The lowest is the stamp at 390×844.
  • the 38px caption title and the white My Daily: at least 6.1:1
  • the 34–58px Wire in #ffc4b0, which counts as large text and needs 3:1: at least 3.99:1

Real NASA photos are darker than pure white, so these are floors. A text shadow adds some extra margin but isn't counted.

Frosted control bar stays readable over anything
Confirmed clean

The bar is 90% --surface with a blur, so up to 10% of whatever scrolls behind it shows through. Worst case: dark ink on the light bar over black reaches 14.05:1, and light ink on the dark bar over white reaches 10.79:1. The chips and buttons inside it are opaque, so their colors are unchanged from 26.html. For example, a muted chip count on a light chip is 4.93:1.

Phones and motion
Confirmed clean

At 390px wide the chips are one swipeable row and the search box takes a full row, so the sticky bar is 169px tall. Before, the chips wrapped to four rows. There is no sideways page scroll at 390px or 1440px (scrollWidth checked). The slow zoom on the hero picture and the lift when hovering a card are turned off under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, and the suite checks that rule exists. The arrow buttons have aria-labels, and focus stays on the pressed arrow after the caption is rebuilt (asserted).

Size of the hero picture not measured
Open

The Image of the Day feed links NASA's full-size uploads, and some are camera originals (for example a …-o.jpg Flickr original). That is what makes the hero sharp, but a single picture could be several MB. This container's proxy refuses www.nasa.gov (CONNECT 403), so the real byte size couldn't be measured. Limits in place: only the current hero picture loads, and the others load only when an arrow is pressed. The same URL was already lazy-loaded by that item's grid card, so the hero adds no new download. If the live page feels slow on mobile data, the fix belongs in the fetcher (prefer a sized variant), not the page. Left open until someone checks the page on a real device.
