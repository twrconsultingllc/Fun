Review #43 — the suit schematic in jarvis-test.html

Session 3 of jarvis/build-plan.html, built in the test copy only. "Show me the suit" opens a generic suit of armour drawn from 31 three.js primitives in glowing wireframe, turning on a turntable while a scanning ring sweeps up and down it. Four HTML callouts (Helmet HUD, Arc reactor, Repulsor, Flight stabilizer) follow the model with leader lines. 01Security

No new network access, and nothing downloaded
Confirmed clean

The plan asked for no downloaded 3D model, to avoid licensing questions and keep the page self-contained. The suit is built in code from boxes, cylinders and icosahedra listed in SUIT_PARTS. A diff of the CSP meta tags of jarvis.html and jarvis-test.html is empty, so connect-src 'self' still holds. The added lines contain 0 fetch(, XMLHttpRequest, import( or src=. In Chromium, both screen sizes ran with no page errors and no Refused CSP messages. jarvis/build-plan.html gains one link, a relative path to this review, and still has 0 scripts, event handlers and forms.

Callout labels are built without HTML strings
Confirmed clean

The labels are <button> elements whose text is set with textContent from the fixed SUIT_CALLOUTS list. The leader lines are SVG elements made with createElementNS, and their positions are set as numbers. A grep of jarvis-test.html still finds 0 innerHTML, insertAdjacentHTML, eval( or new Function. Spec lines are fixed strings that go through say() and add(), which use textContent. Nothing the user types reaches the scene: the command only picks between 'assemble' and null.

The labels go away with the scene
Confirmed clean

The labels are HTML outside the WebGL canvas, so closeHolo() now calls a scene's optional dispose(), which removes them. In Chromium, both "close" and Escape left no #suit-labels in the page. Without WebGL, the suite checks that project({kind:'suit'}) says so and creates no labels.

02Code quality & accessibility

Callouts stay on screen at 390×844, measured
Confirmed clean

As review 38 did for the HUD, a Chromium script measured every label's box 24 times over 18 s, which is more than a full turn of the turntable, at 1280×800 and 390×844. It compared each box with the screen, with the bottom of the top HUD and its buttons (93 px on desktop, 104 px on the phone) and with the top of the caption and text box (639 px and 662 px). It found 0 labels off screen, under the HUD or under the text box, and 0 overlaps.

The layout is a pure function, layoutCallouts. It puts a column of labels down each side of the figure, each label level with its anchor where there's room. Labels are stacked so they don't overlap and clamped to the free area. A label within 24 px of the middle keeps the side it had last frame, so it doesn't flicker as the suit turns. The suite runs it on 300 random seeded poses at phone size and checks that every label fits and none overlap. It also checks a pile-up of all four labels below the free area, the side rules, and that a label sits level with its anchor (anchor at 400 px and label 24 px tall gives a top of 388 px).

Two problems found in the screenshots, both fixed
Fixed
  • During "suit up", the labels and the repulsor glows showed before their parts had arrived, so leader lines pointed at empty space. Now the labels and glows appear only once the suit is together.
  • On the desktop the turntable sat under the caption box. The figure now fits in the space between the top HUD and the bottom 350 px, and is lifted 60 px. On the phone the boots are clear of the caption. The turntable ring can still pass under a three-line caption, which is fine because the caption box is opaque enough to read.
Contrast of the labels, including dimmed ones
Fixed

A label's anchor can be on the back of the suit, so its leader line dims to 0.3 and the label dims too. Labels are #39ff88 on rgba(2,10,6,.85). Worked out from the WCAG formula: 15.12:1 over the dark scene, and 10.36:1 even over pure white. The selected label (#ffc24a) is 12.48:1 and 8.55:1. A dimmed label at the first opacity I tried, 0.7, fell to 4.16:1 over white, which can happen behind it when the repulsors fire their near-white beams. At 0.8 it's 5.61:1, so that's the value used. 0.75 would give 4.82:1, but that leaves less margin. Labels are real buttons, so they can be reached with Tab and get the page's amber focus ring.
