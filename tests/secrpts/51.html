01Security & privacy

Always listening is opt-in, disclosed, and never left on
Confirmed clean

The switch starts off on every visit and nothing about it is stored: the suite checks localStorage has no wake or listen key after switching it on. While it's on, a note under the input says, in amber, that everything the mic hears goes to the browser's online speech service until it's turned off, and that it works in Chrome and Edge only. Jarvis says the same when it's switched on. The mic button gets an amber ring, which stays visible over the projector (checked in Chromium: the ring is the topmost element at both sizes). It turns itself off when the tab is hidden, when the browser refuses the mic, and when recognition ends more than five times in ten seconds, instead of retrying in a loop. Where it isn't supported (no speech recognition, or no Chromium userAgentData, as in Safari and Firefox) the switch is hidden and asking for it says why.

It still never opens a second mic stream
Confirmed clean

Always listening is the same speech recognition object with continuous set, not a new stream. A grep finds two getUserMedia( calls in the page, both video-only (hand control and the threat scan). That keeps the rule from review 41: nothing opens the mic while recognition is running.

Jarvis can't wake himself
Fixed

With recognition running all the time, it hears Jarvis's own answers. Anything heard while speech synthesis is speaking is ignored, and everything else has to start with the wake word, so an answer that mentions "Jarvis" mid-sentence can't trigger a command. Tested with a fake that reports speaking.

The briefing reads only this site, and headlines stay text
Confirmed clean

"Brief me" fetches daily-wire/feeds.json by relative URL, which connect-src 'self' already allows; the CSP meta tag is unchanged. In Chromium the only new request was to 127.0.0.1:8765/daily-wire/. Headlines come from outside feeds, so they're treated as untrusted: they go into the chat log through textContent (in add()) and into speech, never into HTML, and are cut to 180 characters. No weather, as the user decided: the page has no geolocation call and no new host, and the suite checks that.

Live-stream spam in the local headlines
Fixed

Running the briefing against the real feeds.json picked a Southwest Florida "headline" that was live-stream bait in styled Unicode letters from a Russian-language site. briefSpam() now skips titles with fullwidth or maths-styled letters, Cyrillic, or "watch live" / "live stream" wording, and the briefing reads the next newest item. Ordinary titles like "Watch: the eclipse in pictures" and "Streaming prices rise again" still pass. The same items still show on My Daily Wire itself; dropping them there would be a change to excludeSources in daily-wire/feeds.config.json, which wasn't part of this session.

02Code quality & accessibility

The fake recognizer behaves like the real one
Confirmed clean

Per CLAUDE.md, the new tests use a fake whose start() throws InvalidStateError while running and whose onstart/onend arrive later. That matters for one path in particular: tapping the mic while always listening is on must not call start() again, because the real one would throw. The tap now means "the next phrase is for Jarvis" with no name needed, and the suite checks the start count doesn't change.

Gesture thresholds pinned, not changed
Confirmed clean

The user reported nothing misfiring and asked to keep the numbers. Seven checks now sit on each edge: a pinch closes below 0.26 of palm size and not at 0.27, stays closed to 0.41 and opens at 0.43, a swipe needs over 22% of the frame within 0.4 s, swipes are 0.9 s apart, and a fist under 150 ms isn't a squeeze. Moving the pinch to 0.28 and the swipe to 0.20 made two of them fail, so they really pin the values.

Readable at both sizes
Confirmed clean

Headless Chromium at 1280×800 and 390×844: the switch fits beside the skin button, the page doesn't scroll sideways (scroll width 1280 and 390), and there were no page errors or CSP violations. Computed contrast for the amber note against the page background: 12.46:1 (Jarvis), 12.90:1 (Matrix), 10.13:1 (Panthers). The switch when on: 15.10:1, 15.18:1 and 7.66:1. It's a real button with aria-pressed, and its label follows the skin ("HEY MORPHEUS").

Not heard on a real device
Open

Headless Chromium has no real speech recognition, so always listening was tested with fakes. Real behaviour to try: how often Chrome ends a continuous session and restarts it, whether Android Chrome plays its listening sound on each restart (it often does), and how well it catches "Jarvis" from across a room.

The briefing is as fresh as the last feed refresh
Open

Headlines come from the file the Daily Wire workflow rebuilds every 6 hours, so they can be up to 6 hours old, and the briefing says so when they're over a day old. That's by design, to keep the page off outside hosts.
