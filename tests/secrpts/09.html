01Root cause & fix

A full-bleed header border overlapped by the disclaimer box's intentional negative margin
Fixed

header.hero has no max-width, so its border-bottom: 1px solid var(--panel-border) spanned the full viewport width, edge to edge. The very next element, .disclaimer, is deliberately pulled up with margin: -1.5rem auto 3rem to sit snugly under the hero instead of leaving a gap. Geometrically, that negative margin moves .disclaimer's top edge 24px above the header's own bottom edge — and since .disclaimer-box (the visible card with its own background and orange border) starts right at that same top edge with no extra spacing, and is definitely taller than 24px, its box always extends past the header's border position. Painted later in DOM order, it draws on top of that stretch of the header's border for its own width (roughly the shared 880px content column) while the header's border remains fully visible in the empty margins on either side, on any viewport wider than the content column. That combination is exactly the reported symptom: a gray line visible across the wide parts of the screen but interrupted where the disclaimer box sits, with a sliver of misalignment right at the box's top edge. Every section border-bottom further down the page, by contrast, lives entirely inside .wrap (max-width 880px) with nothing before or after it doing a negative-margin overlap, which is why those read as clean.

Fixed: removed border-bottom: 1px solid var(--panel-border); from header.hero. The disclaimer box already carries its own visible border for separation, and the header's linear-gradient(180deg, var(--bg-2), var(--bg) 85%) background already fades toward the page background before it reaches the bottom, so no seam is needed there — deleting the line removes the stray full-width bar without touching the deliberate tight overlap between the hero and the disclaimer, or any of the page's other borders.
