02Code quality & accessibility

Picking a voice changed nothing on Android
Fixed

Android Chrome uses an utterance's lang to choose its voice and largely ignores voice when the two don't match. say() set only voice, so every pick came out in the phone's default English. It now sets lang to the chosen voice's language as well. That's harmless on iPhone, Mac, Edge and desktop Chrome, where the voice already carries that language. The new checks fail against the previous page (6 failures) and pass now.

The menu listed languages, sometimes twice
Fixed

Android gives web pages one voice per language and accent, named after it ("English United States"), and sometimes lists it twice, on-device and online. Those names are the voices; the menu showed what the phone offers. It now lists each name once and shortens them to "English (US)", "English (UK)", "English (India)" so they fit at phone width. The first entry is "🔊 Auto · …", which saves the room the word "VOICE:" took up. Checked in headless Chromium at 390 px with an Android-style voice list: the label fits beside the skin button and the page doesn't scroll sideways.

Accent voices keep the skin's full pitch and speed
Fixed

Review 47 gave any voice picked from the menu the gentler pitch meant for character voices. On Android, where every choice is an accent voice, that made Morpheus and Stanley sound almost like Jarvis. The gentler pitch now applies only to a voice on that skin's own list (Ralph for Morpheus, for example). Any other voice keeps the skin's full pitch and speed: Morpheus 0.55 and 0.86, Stanley 1.2 and 1.12.

Android can't offer more voices than this
Open

On Android, the web only gets one voice per accent. Which one it is (male, female, or a particular style) is set in the phone's own text-to-speech settings (Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech, or Google's "Speech Services" app), not by the page. So on Android the skins differ by accent, pitch and speed. Distinct character voices there would need an online voice service (option 3 in the earlier discussion). Not verified on a real Android phone: headless Chromium has no voices.
