Session 1 · smallPolish and voice-reactive orb

Status: DoneNeeds: nothingReview: next number in tests/secrpts/
  1. Claude Orb reacts to your voice. While the mic is listening, open an audio stream (getUserMedia({audio:true})) into a Web Audio AnalyserNode and drive energy from its volume, so the rings jump as you talk. Close the stream when listening ends. Speech synthesis has no audio tap, so while Jarvis speaks, pulse on each word with the utterance's onboundary event, and fall back to the current sine pulse where that event doesn't fire.
  2. Claude Mic note. One line by the mic, shown once: voice input uses the browser's online speech service, and typing stays on the device (from review 36).
  3. Claude Hint backing strip. A translucent strip behind the projector's top HUD text, so a bright star can't wash it out (from review 37).
  4. Claude Reduced motion. Hold the orb's glow and ring sizes steady when reduce is set (from review 36).
  5. You Talk to it on your phone and say whether the orb's reaction feels right.

Checks: the suite covers the volume-to-energy mapping as a pure function. In Chromium, feed --use-file-for-fake-audio-capture a WAV and confirm the energy rises. Screenshot the HUD over the galaxy core.

Session notes

Done 2026-10-09 in commit 09b0ced, reviewed in review 40. Suite: 145 checks, up from 104.

Orb. Loudness maps on a decibel scale, −50 dB to 0 and −12 dB to 1 (levelToEnergy), with a floor of 0.2 while listening so the rings never go dead. It rises fast and settles slowly. In Chromium a generated WAV (quiet, then a loud tone) held the energy at 0.20, then 0.92–1.00, at both screen sizes. The level stream is opened from recognition's onstart, not the tap, and closed in onend/onerror. A stream that arrives after listening ended is stopped at once. Word pulses could not be seen headless, because there are no voices there, and Chrome's online voices don't fire onboundary, so the sine fallback is what most people will see.

Divergences from the plan. Under reduced motion the level stream isn't opened at all, since the orb holds still anyway. If recognition reports audio-capture while the level stream is open, the page stops the stream and doesn't open it again that visit. That guards against phones where the two can't share the mic. The mic note shows from page load (wherever speech recognition exists) rather than on the first tap, so it's read before any audio goes out. It stops showing once the mic has been used on that device. The HUD strip is one #holo-top wrapper around title, stat and hint, at 90% opacity: that is the lowest opacity that keeps the hint above 4.5:1 even over pure white (5.30:1).

Still open. Whether a phone lets speech recognition and the level stream share the mic. That's the You step above: if the mic stops understanding you on the phone, say so, and the level stream can be turned off on phones. The test README's assertion total is stale (see review 40).

Session 2 · small–mediumNeural network

Status: Not startedNeeds: nothing
  1. Claude A neural scene. Layers of glowing nodes joined by faint lines, with pulses travelling along the connections. "Show me your brain" or "neural network" opens it.
  2. Claude It thinks when Jarvis thinks. While mode is think, a burst of pulses fires from the input layer to the output layer. Each answer lights the output nodes. With the scene closed, this does nothing.
  3. Claude Hand control: pick a node to have Jarvis explain what a layer does (kid-friendly), next to rebuild with a different shape, squeeze to fire every node at once.

Checks: commands in the suite, and Chromium screenshots at desktop and phone width while it's thinking.

Session 3 · mediumIron Man suit schematic

Status: Not startedNeeds: nothing
  1. Claude A suit scene: a generic armoured figure built from three.js primitives in glowing wireframe, on a turntable with a scanning line. It should be a look-alike, not a copy of Marvel's design, and use no downloaded 3D model. That avoids licensing questions and keeps the page self-contained.
  2. Claude Callouts: HTML labels projected from 3D points ("Repulsor", "Arc reactor", "Flight stabilizer", "Helmet HUD"), with leader lines that follow the model as it turns. Tapping or pinching a part gives a fun spec line.
  3. Claude Voice: "Suit up" and "show me the suit" open it, and "suit up" plays a short assembly animation with pieces flying into place. A fist squeeze fires the repulsors.

Checks: callout positions stay on screen at 390×844 (measure the boxes in Chromium, as review 38 did for the HUD). Suite checks for the commands and the part list.

Session 4 · mediumEarth globe

Status: Not startedNeeds: nothing
  1. Claude Land outlines: from Natural Earth's public-domain 110 m data (the world-atlas npm package carries it; npm is reachable from claude.ai/code). Convert it once into a small embedded outline, or a file under jarvis/, so the page fetches nothing from outside. NASA's image servers are blocked from the container, so don't plan on Blue Marble textures.
  2. Claude A globe scene: a dark ocean sphere with glowing coastlines, a day/night line from the real time, city lights from an embedded list of about 200 large cities, and a few satellites in orbit.
  3. Claude "Show me Florida": an embedded table of places (countries, US states, big cities) with latitude and longitude. The camera flies there and a marker pulses. "Show me Earth" opens the globe, and next tours the continents.

Checks: a lat/lon-to-3D unit test (for example, Florida faces the camera after "show me Florida"), plus screenshots.

Session 6 · mediumWake word, briefing, gesture tuning

Status: Not startedNeeds: you to have tried hand control on your own devices
  1. Claude "Hey Jarvis": an "always listening" switch that keeps speech recognition running and acts only on phrases that start with "Jarvis". It's off by default and resets each visit. The switch says plainly that the audio goes to the browser's speech service, and that it works in Chrome and Edge only. Where it isn't supported, the switch is hidden.
  2. Claude "Brief me": reads the time, then the top headline from each of a few categories in daily-wire/feeds.json. That file is on this site, so connect-src 'self' already allows it. It's spoken and shown in the log.
  3. You Weather is your call. It needs an outside service (for example Open-Meteo, which needs no key), which means adding that host to connect-src and asking for your location. Say yes or no before this session. Without a yes, the briefing skips weather.
  4. You Gesture feedback. Before this session, try hand control and note what misfired: pinches not registering, accidental swipes, a fist read as a pinch, lag on which device.
  5. Claude Gesture tuning from that feedback. The thresholds are in createGestures and classifyHand (pinch below 0.26 and released above 0.42 of palm size, a swipe of 22% of the frame in 0.4 s, a 150 ms fist). Add a synthetic-landmark test for each reported misfire before changing a number.
