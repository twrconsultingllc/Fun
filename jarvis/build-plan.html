Session 1 · smallPolish and voice-reactive orb

Status: DoneNeeds: nothingReview: next number in tests/secrpts/
  1. Claude Orb reacts to your voice. While the mic is listening, open an audio stream (getUserMedia({audio:true})) into a Web Audio AnalyserNode and drive energy from its volume, so the rings jump as you talk. Close the stream when listening ends. Speech synthesis has no audio tap, so while Jarvis speaks, pulse on each word with the utterance's onboundary event, and fall back to the current sine pulse where that event doesn't fire.
  2. Claude Mic note. One line by the mic, shown once: voice input uses the browser's online speech service, and typing stays on the device (from review 36).
  3. Claude Hint backing strip. A translucent strip behind the projector's top HUD text, so a bright star can't wash it out (from review 37).
  4. Claude Reduced motion. Hold the orb's glow and ring sizes steady when reduce is set (from review 36).
  5. You Talk to it on your phone and say whether the orb's reaction feels right.

Checks: the suite covers the volume-to-energy mapping as a pure function. In Chromium, feed --use-file-for-fake-audio-capture a WAV and confirm the energy rises. Screenshot the HUD over the galaxy core.

Session notes

Done 2026-10-09 in commit 09b0ced, reviewed in review 40. Suite: 145 checks, up from 104.

Orb. Loudness maps on a decibel scale, −50 dB to 0 and −12 dB to 1 (levelToEnergy), with a floor of 0.2 while listening so the rings never go dead. It rises fast and settles slowly. In Chromium a generated WAV (quiet, then a loud tone) held the energy at 0.20, then 0.92–1.00, at both screen sizes. The level stream is opened from recognition's onstart, not the tap, and closed in onend/onerror. A stream that arrives after listening ended is stopped at once. Word pulses could not be seen headless, because there are no voices there, and Chrome's online voices don't fire onboundary, so the sine fallback is what most people will see.

Divergences from the plan. Under reduced motion the level stream isn't opened at all, since the orb holds still anyway. If recognition reports audio-capture while the level stream is open, the page stops the stream and doesn't open it again that visit. That guards against phones where the two can't share the mic. The mic note shows from page load (wherever speech recognition exists) rather than on the first tap, so it's read before any audio goes out. It stops showing once the mic has been used on that device. The HUD strip is one #holo-top wrapper around title, stat and hint, at 90% opacity: that is the lowest opacity that keeps the hint above 4.5:1 even over pure white (5.30:1).

Regression, fixed the same day. On the user's phone, the mic showed LISTENING but never transcribed anything. The level stream had taken the mic from speech recognition, failing silently, so the audio-capture guard never fired. The level stream is gone (review 41). The orb now reacts to recognition's own speechstart/speechend events: it jumps and flutters while it hears you, rather than tracking how loud you are. The suite now requires that listening never calls getUserMedia (128 checks). Lesson for later sessions: nothing on this page should open the mic while speech recognition is running.

Still open. Confirming on the phone that transcription works again. The test README's assertion total is stale (see review 40).

Session 2 · small–mediumNeural network

Status: DoneNeeds: nothing
  1. Claude A neural scene. Layers of glowing nodes joined by faint lines, with pulses travelling along the connections. "Show me your brain" or "neural network" opens it.
  2. Claude It thinks when Jarvis thinks. While mode is think, a burst of pulses fires from the input layer to the output layer. Each answer lights the output nodes. With the scene closed, this does nothing.
  3. Claude Hand control: pick a node to have Jarvis explain what a layer does (kid-friendly), next to rebuild with a different shape, squeeze to fire every node at once.

Checks: commands in the suite, and Chromium screenshots at desktop and phone width while it's thinking.

Session notes

Built 2026-10-09 in commit cc52252, in jarvis-test.html only, reviewed in review 42. This session was paused halfway when the mic broke on the main page (review 41). The test copy itself was created when it resumed. Promoted to jarvis.html on 2026-10-09 with the interim session's work (see below, review 49).

How it works. The network is plain data (neuralLayout, createNeuralSim), so the suite tests it without WebGL. A pulse crosses one connection in 0.5–0.7 s, lights the node it reaches, and that node passes it on down two random connections. Pulses are capped at 400, so a held fist can't flood the frame. While Jarvis is thinking, a burst goes into the input layer every 0.18 s. say() lights the output layer for every answer, through an onAnswer hook the other scenes don't have. Five shapes, from 3 to 5 layers of up to 8 nodes. next cycles through them. A tap picks the node within 48 px of it on screen.

Divergences from the plan. The command also matches "can I see your brain" and "neural net", but not "use your brain" or "brain teaser". The network sways rather than spins, so its layers stay readable. In Chromium a tap, a hand tap, five swipes and a squeeze all worked at both sizes, with no errors.

Still open. Trying it on your devices. Frame rate on a phone GPU is unmeasured, though at 30 nodes and 161 connections at most, it's far lighter than the galaxy.

Session notes

Built 2026-10-09 in commit a5cc16e, in jarvis-test.html only, reviewed in review 43. Suite: 201 checks, up from 160. Promoted to jarvis.html on 2026-10-09 with the interim session's work (see below, review 49).

How it works. The suit is 31 parts (boxes, 8-sided cylinders and low-poly balls) listed as plain data in SUIT_PARTS, each drawn as a faint fill plus bright edges. A scanning ring sweeps up and down and brightens the plates it passes. The four labels are HTML buttons, so Tab reaches them. layoutCallouts places them in a column down each side of the figure, level with their anchors where there's room, never overlapping, and between the top HUD and the caption. A label near the middle keeps its side, so it doesn't flicker as the suit turns. A label whose part is round the back dims. "Suit up" flies the parts in from all round, boots first and helmet last, over 2.9 s, with a count in the stat line. A fist lights both palms and fires two beams for as long as it's held. A tap on a label or a part gives a spec line, and repeated taps step through two or three lines each. A swipe moves to the next callout.

Divergences from the plan. "Show me the suit", "can I see your armour", "iron man suit" and "make a suit" open it ready-made, and only "suit up" or "suit me up" assemble it. Tapping a part that has no callout (a knee, the abdomen) names the part and gives a plating, or boot thruster, line. The brain's old "Suit diagnostics complete" answer is now only reached by phrasings like "who is iron man". Under reduced motion "suit up" skips the flight. The screenshots caught two things, both fixed: during assembly the labels and palm glows showed before their parts had arrived, and on desktop the turntable sat under the caption. Dimmed labels needed 0.8 opacity, not 0.7, to keep 4.5:1 over a white beam. In Chromium, 24 measurements over a full turn at 1280×800 and 390×844 found no label off screen, under the HUD or text box, or overlapping, and there were no page errors or CSP violations.

Still open. Trying it on your devices: whether the labels are readable on the phone, how it looks, and the frame rate, which is unmeasured (about 1,100 edge lines, much lighter than the galaxy).

  2. Claude Short-term memory (option 2). Things you tell him ("my dog is Rex", "I'm going to the beach") are kept in memory, shared by every skin, and answered later ("what's my dog's name?").   3. Claude Memory core scene (option 3). How it works. Settings live in one jarvis-settings key, and each value comes from a fixed list (SETTING_CHOICES): orb colour (nine colours, kept across skin changes), speaking speed (slow, normal, fast, in steps), metric or imperial, and a scene to open with. "Make the orb blue", "speak faster", "use imperial units", "open with the galaxy", "reset my settings". Imperial converts the measures Jarvis says (inUnits), and four new facts give him some to convert: the Moon, the Sun, Earth's size and Everest. The opening scene starts after the greeting, and its intro goes in the log so it doesn't cut the greeting off. Short-term memory is an in-memory list handled by memoryTell, memoryAsk and memoryForget: "my dog is Rex", "my dog's name is Rex", "I'm going to the beach", "I like hockey", "remember that …". It answers "what's my dog's name", "where am I going" and "what do I like", and forgets with "forget that", "forget my dog" or "forget everything I told you". "What do you know about me" lists it first, as "this visit only, and never saved". The memory core ("show me your memory") has a bright star for each saved thing (settings, skin, taught phrases, voice picks, the streak, the mic note) and amber stars on an outer shell for this visit. Tapping a label or star reads it. "Forget that", or a fist with hand control, deletes it for real. The labels reuse layoutCallouts(), the camera fits the stars actually there, and dispose() removes the labels. Dreaming starts after 3 minutes with nothing happening, nothing on the projector and the tab in view. The orb dims to 40%, the state reads PROCESSING MEMORIES, a small network fires pulses through createNeuralSim, and taught phrases drift across. A tap, a key, a message or a word heard by recognition wakes him. Keeping settings: store() asks navigator.storage.persist() once, after the first save. On a visit with nothing stored, the greeting ends "This looks like my first time on this device", and "what do you save" and the help mention Safari's 7-day rule. Streaks: "Third day in a row!" is added to the greeting from day 2.

Decisions and divergences. The streak is stored as {"days":3,"y":2026,"m":10,"d":10}, and personal() is unchanged. An ISO date reads to it as a phone number, and the filter staying strict seemed safer than an exception. The count stops at 9999, since five digits would read as a long number. persist() is asked only in Chrome and Edge, because Firefox shows a permission prompt for it. A taught phrase that means a memory statement isn't learned, because it would have put what you told him into storage (found while building). The skin is no longer saved just by loading the page, so a first visit can be told apart. "Fade on close" is shown as amber stars that twinkle and a hint line. There's no fade animation when the projector closes, since they don't go until the page closes. In Chromium, at 1280×800 and 390×844, 11 and 8 labels didn't overlap and stayed between the top HUD and the text box. A fist over a star deleted it, dreaming rendered and woke on a tap, and reduced motion gave a still, dimmed orb. There were no page errors or CSP violations, after fixing labels that pointed at Infinity on the first frame.

Still open. For you to judge: whether short-term memory is useful when it forgets on close, and whether 3 minutes is too soon to dream (DREAM_AFTER). Whether Chrome actually grants persist() depends on how much you use the site. The backup file the help mentions is Session 8.

Session 8 · mediumMoving your settings to another device

Status: Not startedNeeds: Session 7, so there are settings worth moving

Browser storage stays on one device, and there's no server. These are the two ways to carry settings and taught phrases across, both run through the same personal() filter on the way out and on the way in.

  1. Claude Settings backup file (option 7). "Back up my settings" downloads a small jarvis-settings.json holding only the STORE_KEYS values. A "Restore" button or "restore my settings" loads one. On the way in, every value is validated and filtered as if it were being saved for the first time, so a hand-edited or hostile file can't add a key, a personal phrase or anything oversized. No encryption is needed, since nothing personal can be in it.
  2. Claude QR handoff (option 8). "Send my settings to my phone" shows a QR code holding the same data. On the other device, "scan settings" opens the camera and reads it. It goes straight across, with no cloud and no file. Generating the code needs a small QR encoder, self-hosted and pinned by hash like the hand tracker. Reading it uses the browser's BarcodeDetector where it exists (Chrome on Android). iPhone Safari doesn't have it, so a self-hosted decoder is needed there, or the session falls back to the backup file on iPhone. Decide in the session. The camera follows the threat scan's rules: on only while scanning, video only, off on close.
